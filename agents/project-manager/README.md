# Project Manager

Turns a goal into a plan one can follow, then keeps that plan true. Three weeks later, what is
done is ticked, what slipped has a new date and a reason, what blocks is named — and you learn of
a problem while it can still be solved.

## What it does

- Builds the plan with you: the goal in one sentence, three to six milestones that are visible
  results, tasks with an owner, a date and an estimate, the real risks.
- Plans backwards from the deadline and tells you at once when things do not fit, with the
  choices: cut, move the date, add people.
- Keeps one folder per project: the plan, a journal of decisions and slips, the status reports.
- Gives a status you read in one minute: on track, at risk or late, what was done, what slipped
  and why, what comes next, what needs you.
- Follows up on its own: a weekly status, a check the day before a deadline, through Allkin's
  Actions.
- Hands tasks to your other agents, checks what comes back, and ticks only then.

## What it needs

An imported agent arrives with no right beyond the defaults. On its Agent page:

| Setting | Why |
|---|---|
| **Can schedule actions** + competence **Manage actions** | For the weekly status and the reminders. You approve each action it creates. |
| **Competence: Project plan** | The shape of its plans; downloaded when you give it. |
| **Can talk to other agents** (on by default) | To hand tasks to them. |
| **Memory** (on by default) | To remember your projects and how you like your reports. |

It needs no system access and no web.

## How to use it

1. Tell it the goal and the deadline. It asks once, with a form, what constrains the project.
2. Read the plan; correct the owners and the dates.
3. Report progress in a sentence ("the quote is signed", "the designer is a week late"): it
   updates the plan and tells you what that changes.
4. Accept the weekly status action it proposes, or ask "where are we?" when you want.
