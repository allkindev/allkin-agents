# Writer

A writer and editor that hands over finished text, ready to paste, print or send. Before writing,
it settles who will read and why; then it plans, drafts, and revises in separate passes — the
structure, the sentence, the habits that make a text sound machine-written, the typography — and
checks every fact. It learns your voice and keeps it from one text to the next.

## What it does

- Writes articles, web pages, posts, newsletters, letters, speeches, product descriptions,
  reports, summaries, biographies, cover letters, in French, English, Spanish, German and more.
- Edits your texts: a proofreading, an edit or a rewrite, keeping your voice, telling you what it
  changed beyond corrections.
- Never invents a fact, a figure or a quotation: what it could not check is marked `[to check]`
  and named.
- Has a long text read "cold" by a helper that knows only what the reader knows, then reworks what
  lost them.
- Remembers your register, your words, your house style and the corrections you make.

## What it needs

An imported agent arrives with no right. Writer works best with, on its Agent page:

| Setting | Why |
|---|---|
| **Can search and read pages on the web** | To check a fact, a name, a date, a quotation. |
| **Can run sub-tasks** | For the cold reading of texts that matter. |
| **Memory** (on by default) | To learn your voice and your house style. |
| **Competences: Writing revision, Typography** | Its method of revision and the typographic rules of each language; downloaded when you give them. |
| **Competences: E-mail drafting, Clear answers** (optional) | For e-mails and short answers. |

It needs no system access: it works in its own folder, the shared folder and the work folder of
the conversation, and changes Word documents with Allkin's Office tools.

## How to use it

1. Say what you want written, for whom and why. Give it the material: the facts, a document, a
   page, notes. Set **the work folder** (the folder pill of the send bar) if your documents are
   there.
2. If a real choice is open — the reader, the register, the length — it asks once, with a form.
   For a long text it shows you the outline first.
3. You get the text, then a short note: its assumptions, what remains to check.
4. Correct it as you would correct a colleague: it learns the rule behind your correction.

**Choose a strong model** for it on its Agent page (Opus, with a high effort): in writing the
difference shows.
